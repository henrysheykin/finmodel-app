# A financial model for investors, with the downside built in

The model behind your pitch deck has one job: to show an investor that the numbers in the deck come from assumptions you understand and can defend. That means a bottom-up revenue build, unit economics that come from the same drivers, a hiring plan, three statements that agree, runway, and a downside case. StartupModel builds that model in Excel from your own numbers, with a low / base / high scenario switch and a Checks sheet. Full costs $349.

This page covers what investors check, and what our model gives you.

## What investors actually check

### 1. A bottom-up revenue build

Investors discount top-down maths ("1% of a large market"). They want to see revenue built from the things you control and can measure:

- **Software:** customers from acquisition and churn, MRR per customer, expansion. See the [SaaS model](/models/saas-financial-model-with-scenarios).
- **E-commerce:** visits × conversion, paid orders from ads budget ÷ CAC, average order value, repeat orders. See the [e-commerce model](/models/ecommerce-financial-model-for-investors).
- **Services:** billable people × utilisation × rate. See the [services model](/models/professional-services-financial-projections).

### 2. Unit economics from the same drivers

CAC, LTV, payback and contribution should be calculated from the numbers that drive revenue and cost, not typed in separately. If an investor changes churn and LTV doesn't move, the model loses credibility. In our SaaS model, blended CAC, LTV, LTV ÷ CAC and CAC payback are live formulas on the model's own drivers.

### 3. Runway and the use of the round

How many months the money lasts, in each scenario. In the SaaS model, net burn per month and runway in months are reported every year, and the round appears as equity raised in the first period, so you can see exactly when cash runs low.

### 4. A hiring plan

Payroll is usually the largest cost of an early company. Investors want roles, start dates and salaries, plus payroll taxes and benefits, not headcount as a percentage of revenue. Every Full model has a headcount sheet built that way.

### 5. Three statements that agree

A P&L alone isn't enough for a serious investor. They expect a cash flow statement and a balance sheet that balance in every period. The Checks sheet in every Full model tests that with live formulas.

### 6. A downside case

Every investor will ask "and if growth is half the plan?" A model with one switch for low, base and high answers that in the meeting. In ours, the low case is ordered the conservative way for every driver: lower growth and conversion, **higher** churn, CAC and costs. The Checks sheet verifies that ordering, so a "downside" can't quietly improve your costs.

## Common mistakes in investor models

1. **Hockey stick without drivers.** Growth that comes from a typed percentage rather than from customers, traffic or people.
2. **Unit economics on a separate tab,** disconnected from the P&L.
3. **No cash flow,** so runway is a guess.
4. **Optimistic downside.** A low case where costs fall faster than revenue.
5. **Five years of monthly detail nobody reads.** Monthly for the period the round covers; annual after that is usually enough.

## How long should the forecast be?

It depends on the round and the investor. A practical approach is monthly detail for the period the round should fund, then annual years after that. The model supports 1, 3 or 5 years in monthly or annual detail; choose what your investors ask for.
