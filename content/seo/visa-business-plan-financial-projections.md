# Five-year financial projections for E-2 business plans

An E-2 business plan has to show that the enterprise is not "marginal": that it can generate more than a minimal living for the investor and their family, or make a significant economic contribution, within about five years. The financial projections carry most of that argument. StartupModel builds them in Excel from the client's numbers: five years of P&L, cash flow and balance sheet, a hiring plan and a low / base / high scenario switch, checked before delivery. This page is written for **business plan writers and immigration attorneys** who prepare these plans for clients.

## What the rules say

The Foreign Affairs Manual, the State Department's guidance for consular officers, defines the test in 9 FAM 402.9-6(E):

- "A marginal enterprise is an enterprise that does not have the present or future capacity to generate enough income to provide more than a minimal living for the treaty investor and their family."
- "An enterprise that does not have the capacity to generate such income but that has a present or future capacity to make a significant economic contribution is not a marginal enterprise."
- "The projected future capacity should generally be realizable within five years from the date the applicant commences normal business activity of the enterprise."

Source: [9 FAM 402.9 — Treaty Traders and Treaty Investors](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040209.html#M402_9_6_B).

That five-year window is why E-2 projections are normally built for five years, and why the numbers have to hang together: a consular officer reading the plan is looking for a business that plausibly grows into its claims.

*This page is general information, not legal advice. The attorney on the case decides what the plan needs.*

## What a strong E-2 projection shows

From the numbers side, a plan that holds up usually shows:

1. **Investment and use of funds that tie out.** The amount invested appears as equity, and what it pays for (fit-out, equipment, inventory, opening cash) appears as capital spending and working capital. If the use-of-funds table and the model disagree, the plan looks careless.
2. **A credible ramp.** Revenue that starts below maturity and grows for stated reasons.
3. **Hiring.** Roles, start dates and salaries by year, which matters both for operating costs and for the economic-contribution argument.
4. **Profit by year five.** Enough to support the non-marginality argument.
5. **Three statements that agree.** P&L, cash flow and a balance sheet that balances every period.
6. **A downside.** What the business looks like if revenue is lower, and whether it still survives.
