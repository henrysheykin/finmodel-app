## How the member base works

The model opens with **members at start**: the presale for a new studio, or the current base for one already trading. Each period:

- **New members** come from marketing (spend ÷ cost per lead × the share of leads that join) plus referrals and walk-ins.
- **Cancellations** are a monthly share of the opening base.
- **Capacity** caps the base. Joins above the cap are counted as *lost to the capacity limit*, the signal to add classes, space or a second site.

Annual models compound the monthly cancellation rate over twelve months and assume the year's new members join mid-year, so annual and monthly versions of the same studio agree closely but not to the dollar.

## Revenue and costs

| Line | How it's modelled |
|---|---|
| Membership fees | Fee × average members; rises once a year with your price increase |
| Joining fees | Fee × members who actually join |
| Personal training | Members buying PT × sessions per month × price per session |
| Retail and drinks | Spend per member per month |
| Group-class instructors | Paid per class, with classes per week an input and wage growth |
| Trainer payouts | % of personal training revenue |
| Staff | Studio manager, front desk and sales, any other role you add |
| Rent, utilities, maintenance, insurance | Monthly inputs |
| Booking software and payment fees | % of revenue |
| Fit-out and equipment | Capital spending in month 1, depreciated over 7 years |

Members pay up front, so receivable days are zero.

## The metrics a lender or partner will ask for

Members at year end, new members, cancellations, joins lost to capacity, capacity used, membership fees per month, revenue split, marketing cost per new member, gross margin, member lifetime value (fee × gross margin ÷ monthly cancellation rate), EBITDA margin and revenue per square foot.

Cancellation rate matters more than any other single input. A small change compounds every month, so flip the scenario switch and watch members and cash.

## What the model doesn't do

- One membership tier and one price: no class packs, annual prepay discounts or freezes.
- No seasonality: January sign-ups and summer slowdowns are not modelled, and the cancellation rate is constant.
- Instructor cost does not change with membership; classes per week is an input.
- No equipment replacement within the horizon; maintenance is an operating cost.

## Common mistakes in studio projections

1. **Ignoring cancellations.** Growth without churn is a fantasy; every month some of the base leaves.
2. **No capacity limit.** A studio cannot sell the 600th membership in a room that fits 450.
3. **Presale treated as permanent.** Test what happens if half the presale members cancel in the first quarter.
4. **Fit-out in the first month's P&L.** It is capital spending, depreciated.
5. **One scenario.** Show the low case before the lender builds their own.

## Questions

**Does it work for a gym with day passes?**
The model is built around memberships. Day passes can be approximated through retail and drinks spend, but are not a separate line.

**Can I model a second location?**
Not as a separate site. Joins lost to capacity show when a second site would be worth testing.
