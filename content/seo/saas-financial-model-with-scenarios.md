## How the revenue build works

**Customers.** New customers each month come from two sources:

- **Organic and referral adds**, entered per month and growing once a year;
- **Paid adds** = paid marketing budget ÷ paid customer acquisition cost (CAC).

Each month: closing customers = opening customers × (1 − monthly churn) + new customers.

**MRR waterfall.** Opening MRR, plus new MRR (new customers × MRR per new customer), plus expansion (a % of opening MRR per month), minus churned MRR, gives closing MRR. MRR per new customer rises once a year by your price increase.

**Revenue** is the average of opening and closing MRR for the period, so customers won or lost during a month count for half of it.

**Deferred revenue.** If some customers pay annually in advance, part of that cash is not yet earned. The model holds a simplified deferred revenue balance on the balance sheet. Its increase adds to operating cash flow, which is why annual billing helps runway.

## The drivers you set

Every driver has a low, base and high value:

| Area | Drivers |
|---|---|
| Customers | Paying customers at start, organic adds per month and their yearly growth, monthly churn |
| Pricing | Average MRR per new customer (ARPA), price increase per year, expansion MRR per month |
| Acquisition | Paid marketing budget per month and its growth, paid CAC |
| Billing | Share of MRR billed annually in advance |
| Cost of revenue | Hosting & payment fees as % of revenue, support & onboarding cost per customer per month |
| Operating costs | Team by role with salaries and start dates, payroll taxes and benefits, software & tools per FTE, office, other G&A |
| Financing | Equity raised, loan amount, rate and term |

For costs and churn, the low case means **higher** values. The Checks sheet verifies that ordering for every driver, because a "downside" with lower churn is a common and misleading mistake.

## The metrics investors ask about

The Dashboard (Full) or P&L sheet (Light) shows, for each year:

- customers and MRR at year end, and ARR (year-end MRR × 12);
- average MRR per customer;
- blended CAC (paid marketing spend ÷ all new customers, organic included);
- LTV (MRR per customer × gross margin ÷ monthly churn) and LTV ÷ CAC;
- CAC payback in months;
- net burn per month and runway in months.

Each is a live formula on the model's own numbers, so it changes when you change an assumption or flip the scenario.

## What a good SaaS model shows a seed investor

1. **A bottom-up revenue build.** Customers and MRR from acquisition, churn and pricing. Not "1% of a $10bn market".
2. **Unit economics that hang together.** CAC, LTV and payback computed from the same drivers that produce revenue, not typed in separately.
3. **Runway in every scenario.** How many months the round buys in the low case, and what has to be true for the high case.
4. **A hiring plan tied to the plan.** Payroll is usually the largest cost line; it should come from roles and start dates, not a % of revenue.
5. **Three statements that agree.** A balance sheet that balances in every month, and cash that reconciles.

## Common mistakes in SaaS models

- **Churn applied to customers but not to MRR,** or vice versa. The waterfall should treat both consistently.
- **CAC that ignores organic customers,** or LTV that ignores gross margin.
- **Annual prepayments counted as revenue** when the cash arrives.
- **Headcount as a % of revenue.** Teams are hired in steps, not percentages.
- **One scenario.** Every investor will ask what happens if growth is half the plan.
