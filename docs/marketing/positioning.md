# Sell proof to buyers who answer to someone

Research date: 2026-10-16 (track M, task M02)

The best buyers are people who have to hand their model to someone who will check it: a lender, an investor, or a client. That makes **three ICPs**. (1) **US small-business loan applicants** (SBA 7(a) and bank), who need projected statements including a balance sheet. (2) **Pre-seed and seed SaaS founders**, who need runway and unit economics with base, downside and upside cases. (3) **Small advisory firms that re-bill clients** (2–10-person accounting and bookkeeping firms, plus visa plan writers as a secondary channel), who need a numbers engine they can trust and resell. All three share one fear, "the numbers are wrong and someone will notice", and the product's architecture answers it directly. The LLM only fills a brief. Deterministic templates write the formulas. An independent challenger must match every scenario-year within $1, and the Checks sheet must not FAIL, or the file is never delivered. The positioning therefore sells **proof, not AI**: "a model built on your numbers, with the arithmetic proven before you get it". Of the 22 names screened, two are real conflicts (Tickmark, Provable). The eight shortlisted below are all CLEAR (thin) or CAUTION, and none has had a legal search. The pricing recommendation is **Light $149 and Full $349**, plus a **5-credit accountant pack at $1,250**. That puts the product clearly above the $99 template shelf where Henry's FinModelsLab sits, and below the $499–$750 entry price for human-built projections. The final price is Henry's decision.

## Three buyers with an external reviewer and a deadline

The ICP choice follows one rule: pick segments where someone outside the buyer will check the model. Verification is worth paying for only when an error has a cost. A founder playing with numbers for themselves will use a free template or a $20/mo AI agent. A borrower whose lender asks for projected balance sheets will not ([Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/loans/small-business/startup-business-loan-documents)).

| | **ICP 1 — Loan applicant (US SBA / bank)** | **ICP 2 — Pre-seed / seed SaaS founder** | **ICP 3 — Advisor who re-bills (small accounting firm; visa plan writer)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job to be done | Give the lender a credible 3-year projection package (monthly year 1, then annual) with a balance sheet, without paying $750+ or waiting weeks | Show runway, burn and unit economics with downside/upside cases when an investor asks for "the model" | Deliver a client forecast (loan, plan, E-2 / Innovator visa) without building it from scratch; keep the work in-house and keep the margin |
| Trigger | Lender or SBA package asks for projections; second location, equipment or acquisition loan | Investor asks for the model after a first meeting; data-room prep | Client's loan or visa application; an advisory engagement the firm would otherwise lose to another provider |
| Must-haves | Full tier: P&L, Cash Flow, Balance Sheet that balances, 12 months monthly + annual years; break-even; explainable assumptions | SaaS template: MRR/ARR, churn, CAC, burn, runway; monthly 24–36 months; one-cell scenario switch; assumptions on their own tab | Editable live formulas; repeatable quality across clients; 5-year horizon and headcount schedule for E-2; volume pricing; ideally white-label (not built yet) |
| Objections | "SCORE/SBDC give a template for free"; "my lender wants a specific format"; "I also need the narrative plan" (not produced) | "Investors don't read models at pre-seed"; "I can prompt Claude or ChatGPT for Excel myself" | "We already have Fathom/Jirav/our own templates"; liability for the numbers; branding; format mismatch with the practice |

| Willingness-to-pay anchor | Free SCORE template → $750 ProjectionHub done-for-you → $2,500–$3,500 plan + projections | DraftPlan $49/$59 and $20/mo AI agents → $1,000–$4,000 freelance model | Fathom $450/mo for 25 companies; E-2 plan about $1,900; re-billed to the client |

**ICP 1 is the largest segment with a hard requirement.** The SBA made **77,600 7(a) loans worth $37B in FY25**, and about **8,900 loans ($5.6B) went to startups** between January and September 2025 ([SBA](https://legacy.sba.gov/article/2025/09/30/trump-sba-delivers-record-capital-small-businesses-fy25)). Startups need projections the most, because they have no operating history. Tim Berry, founder of LivePlan, writes that mainstream SBA programmes expect **at least 12 months of monthly projections**, with annual years 2–3 ([timberry.com](https://timberry.com/business-plan-financials-how-many-months-years/)). The source is secondary and dated 2016, and the SBA SOP wording was not checked. That shape is exactly the Full tier's monthly-then-annual 3-year build. Light does not fit this buyer, because a single P&L sheet has no balance sheet. The free SCORE template, which includes diagnostic checks ([SCORE](https://www.score.org/resource/template/financial-projections-template)), sets the floor. ProjectionHub's **$750 done-for-you projections** and its claim of "thousands of SBA loans approved" set the ceiling for the comparison ([ProjectionHub](https://projectionhub.com/pricing)). The UK Start Up Loans scheme requires a **12-month cash-flow forecast** with every application ([Start Up Loans](https://www.startuploans.co.uk/cash-flow-forecast/)). But those loans are capped at **£25,000** and come with a free official template ([Start Up Loans media centre](https://www.startuploans.co.uk/media-centre/uk-business-owners-continue-to-start-up-and-secure-early-stage-financing)), so the UK buyer is a weaker, more price-sensitive version of ICP 1, not a separate ICP.

**ICP 2 has the most free substitutes, but it is the best fit for the scenario switch.** A model is not an entry ticket: Y Combinator says most funded companies have no revenue, and its FAQ does not mention financial models ([YC FAQ](https://ycombinator.com/faq)). When an investor does ask, the expected content matches the SaaS template closely. A vendor guide lists 18–24 months of runway, a bottom-up revenue build, assumptions on their own tab, **at least three scenarios (base, downside, upside)** and "no fifty-tab spreadsheet" ([Forecastr](https://forecastr.com/blog/startup-financial-model-pre-seed)). The main competition is the founder's own AI agent. Ankura tested five AI Excel tools and found that **"no tool produced a production-ready model"** ([Ankura](https://ankura.com/insights/putting-ai-to-the-test-how-5-tools-performed-across-3-financial-models)). That finding is the wedge for this ICP: the pitch is "no prompting, no auditing", not "AI builds Excel". Finmark's shutdown on 1 April 2026 ([Metapraxis](https://metapraxis.com/finmark-alternatives)) and Runway's repricing as cfo.ai at $375–$1,500/mo ([cfo.ai](https://cfo.ai/pricing)) give a factual reason to want a file you own.

**ICP 3 is a smaller segment, but its members buy repeatedly, and the demand signal is unusually direct.** In a 2026 survey of 500 UK businesses, cash-flow planning and forecasting was the advisory service most often bought from the primary accounting firm (52%). More than a third of clients buy it elsewhere. Leakage was highest at **firms with 2–10 staff (48%)**. Price was not cited as a reason, and **94% would bring the work back** if quality matched ([Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/a-third-of-accounting-clients-advisory-work-is-going-to-rival-firms-u-k-study-finds/)). The survey was commissioned by a vendor that sells to accountants, so treat it as directional. On Capterra, a CPA asked LivePlan for pricing aimed at people who make **"five or less" projections a year** ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/142049/LivePlan/reviews/)). That request is the basis for the credit pack below. Visa plan writers belong in this ICP as a channel, not as end buyers. E-2 plans are judged against a test of whether the business can support more than a minimal living within five years ([RJ Immigration Law](https://rjimmigrationlaw.com/resources/what-is-the-marginal-requirement-for-an-e-2-investor-visa/), citing [9 FAM 402.9](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040209.html#M402_9_6_B)). Plans need 5-year P&L, balance-sheet and cash-flow projections and typically cost about **$1,900** ([BSB Consulting](https://www.bsbcon.com/us/blog/how-much-does-a-business-plan-for-an-e-2-visa-cost/)). The visa buyer also needs a narrative plan, which the product does not write. Selling to the writer, who already has the narrative, avoids that objection. White-label output and any evidence that firms buy Excel models rather than SaaS are both **untested hypotheses**.

Two segments were left out on purpose. Existing SMB owners planning an expansion or a refinance are probably the majority of SBA borrowers, but this run found no sources on their triggers, so they are folded into ICP 1 until researched. Hobbyists and students would buy only at DraftPlan prices.

## Positioning statement

> For small-business owners, founders and their advisors who must hand a financial model to a lender, investor or client, **[NAME]** is a custom Excel financial model built from their own key numbers, with every formula live and the arithmetic independently recomputed before delivery, that lets them send the file with confidence and change it themselves. Unlike templates they must fit to their business by hand, AI spreadsheet agents whose output they must audit, or freelancers who take weeks and charge thousands, [NAME] will not deliver a model unless its in-file checks pass and an independent recompute matches it to the dollar in every scenario and year.

Two guardrails. First, the "Unlike" clause describes how alternatives work, not their quality. Copy must never say templates are "generic" or "wrong", because that also runs down Henry's FinModelsLab catalogue (see `docs/marketing/competitors.md`). Second, "to the dollar" rests on the $1 tolerance in the challenger. If that tolerance ever changes, this wording must change with it.

## Message house: one promise, three provable pillars

**Umbrella message:** *A financial model built on your numbers, with the arithmetic proven before you get it.*

Every proof point below is a product rule from `CLAUDE.md`, the backlog (`docs/BACKLOG.md`, E02), or a measured result of the prototype in `poc/`. It is not a statistic about customers. The product has no customers, testimonials or user counts yet, and copy must not suggest otherwise. A proof point goes into public copy only once it is true of the shipped templates, not just the cafe prototype.

|---|---|---|---|

| Proof points (true product facts) | Wizard covers business type, goal, stage, horizon (1, 3 or 5 years), monthly or annual granularity, and a key-numbers step. Every driver has a Low, Base and High value. Templates for SaaS, cafe/restaurant, retail/e-commerce, rental real estate and professional services, each with its own driver logic (e.g. MRR/churn/CAC for SaaS; rent roll, DSCR and IRR for rental) | The AI never writes workbook formulas; it only fills a validated brief, and deterministic templates write the formulas. ICP emphasis: loan applicants lead with Pillar 2 (balance sheet balances, checks pass). Founders lead with Pillar 3 (scenario switch, ownership after the SaaS shutdowns). Advisors lead with Pillars 2 and 3 together (a file they can put their name to and edit). Delivery time stays a placeholder, [DELIVERY TIME], until the worker's real numbers exist.

## Price Light at $149 and Full at $349, decision Henry's

Four anchors set the window. **DraftPlan charges $49/$59**, and general AI agents start at **$20/mo** ([getdraftplan.com](https://getdraftplan.com/), [GPT for Work](https://gptforwork.com/blog/shortcut-ai-alternatives)). **FinModelsLab sells about 2,000 templates at a flat $99** ([products.json](https://finmodelslab.com/products.json?limit=250)), and marketplace templates run **$35–$210** ([eFM](https://www.efinancialmodels.com/downloads/saas-financial-model-excel-template-fully-vetted-and-ready-to-use-145016/)). Entry-level human work starts at **Someka's $250 customization** ([Someka](https://www.someka.net/excel-template/restaurant-financial-plan/)), with **ProjectionHub+ at $499 and done-for-you projections at $750** ([ProjectionHub](https://projectionhub.com/pricing)). Freelance models run **$1,000–$4,000 over 1–4 weeks** ([eFM survey](https://www.efinancialmodels.com/?p=659204)). The M01 conclusion still holds: price clearly above $99 to avoid cannibalising FinModelsLab and to signal "step up from a template", and below the $250–$750 human entry band so that it reads as "this instead of hiring someone".

**Full at $349** is the product's centre of gravity, because every ICP needs the three statements. It costs **less than half of the $750 done-for-you price** and about a third of the cheapest freelance build, so a loan applicant or a firm re-billing a client can buy without deliberating. At the same time it is 3.5x a FinModelsLab template, a gap wide enough that a template shopper does not see it as an alternative to a $99 file. It sits above Someka's $250, but Someka's $250 buys customization of a template, not a three-statement model with a balance-sheet check. $299 is a defensible alternative if early conversion is weak. Going above $399 starts to collide with ProjectionHub+ at $499, whose "CPA developed" and SBA claims we cannot yet match with a track record.

**Light at $149** is the hardest call. Light is one P&L sheet plus Inputs and Checks, which is *fewer sheets* than a $99 FinModelsLab file that advertises a dashboard and a 5-year monthly forecast. Pricing Light under $99 would cannibalise FinModelsLab and signal "cheap template". Pricing it at $99 is the worst option, because it competes head-on for the same search click. At $149 Light sells only what templates do not offer: your numbers, the switch and the proof. Its main job is to make Full look like the obvious choice, since the extra $200 buys the cash flow, balance sheet, headcount and dashboard. Offer a **Light-to-Full upgrade for the price difference** within a fixed window (e.g. 30 days), so Light works as a low-risk first purchase rather than a dead end. If Henry wants Light to win on volume, $129 is the floor; anything lower brings the substitution problem back.

**Accountant credit pack: 5 Full credits for $1,250** ($250 per model, about 28% off). Credits are valid for 12 months and usable on any template. The pack size comes straight from the CPA's "five or less" request ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/142049/LivePlan/reviews/)). At $250 per model, a firm that re-bills $500–$1,000 for a client forecast keeps a healthy margin. The total stays well below one year of Fathom at $450/mo ([Fathom](https://www.fathomhq.com/pricing)), which gives the firm an easy internal comparison. A 10-pack can wait until someone uses up a 5-pack. White-label output is the obvious upsell for this pack, but it is not built and should not be promised yet. A separate decision for Henry: whether FinModelsLab buyers get their **$99 credited** toward a Full model. That would turn the overlap with FinModelsLab into a funnel, but it ties the two brands together publicly.

All prices stay **[PRICE] in product copy** until Henry decides. The numbers above are a recommendation built from public anchors, not from any test of willingness to pay.

## Open questions

The SBA SOP 50 10 8 wording on monthly versus annual projections was not checked in the primary source. ICP 1's must-haves rest on a 2016 secondary source and on Bankrate. No keyword volumes were pulled, so every "where they search" entry is a hypothesis. The next step is to run Ahrefs on "SBA loan projections", "E-2 visa financial projections", "startup financial model" and "cash flow forecast template". It is unknown whether small accounting firms buy Excel models at all, rather than SaaS, and whether they need white-label output. Five firm interviews would settle both questions faster than more desk research. Existing SMB owners planning an expansion or refinance, and rental investors whose lenders ask for DSCR, were not researched and may deserve their own ICP. The UK Start Up Loans job needs a 12-month cash-flow forecast, which Light does not include. Henry needs to decide whether that buyer is served by Full or by a future cash-flow Light. Light's position against FinModelsLab's richer $99 files needs real price testing. The figures here have not been validated with any buyer. Every candidate name still needs Henry's TMview, USPTO and WIPO search in classes 9/35/36/42 and a domain check. Tickmark's vendor and the Worthware/"Cellwise" mark were not confirmed. Finally, every message-house proof point must be re-verified against the shipped templates before launch. Today only the cafe prototype has passed the challenger.

## Conclusion

The research changes what is being sold. The deliverable is not really "an Excel model", because templates and AI agents make those almost free. What the buyer pays for is the right to hand a file to a sceptical reader without first auditing it themselves. That is why the ICPs are defined by who reviews the model rather than by industry, why the name shortlist leans on "proof", and why the price belongs above the template shelf and well below human labour. The next tests are a sample file with published Checks and challenger results, five accountant interviews, and a keyword pull. Each one is cheaper than launching on assumptions, and together they will show whether $349 is too low as well as whether it is too high.
