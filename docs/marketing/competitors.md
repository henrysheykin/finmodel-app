# Verified custom models beat a crowded field

Research date: 2026-10-16 (track M, task M01)

Nobody in the market sells what Ledgerline plans to sell: a model built from the buyer's own numbers through a short wizard, delivered as a live-formula `.xlsx` with a one-cell Low/Base/High switch, an in-file Checks sheet and an independent recompute, for a one-time price. The competition splits into four groups, and each one misses at least one of those pieces. **AI model builders** are cheap and fast (free to $59 per model, or $4.99–$125/mo) but show little proof that the numbers are right. Independent tests found that **no general AI Excel agent produced a production-ready model** in one pass ([Ankura](https://ankura.com/insights/putting-ai-to-the-test-how-5-tools-performed-across-3-financial-models)). **Template sellers** cluster at **$35–$210 per file**, but the files are generic and buyers say so in reviews. **Planning SaaS** costs $15–$1,500/mo, keeps the model inside the vendor's app, and several founder tools shut down in 2025–26. **Human builders** do fit the buyer's business, but they start at $750 for done-for-you projections and run to **$1,000–$4,000 and 1–4 weeks** for a freelancer's 3-statement model. The biggest strategic risk is internal. Henry's own **FinModelsLab** sells about 2,000 industry templates at a flat **$99** and advertises the same headline features (5-year monthly forecast, dashboard, Low/Base/High scenarios). Ledgerline therefore needs a price and a message that make it the step up from FinModelsLab, not a rival to it. Unless stated otherwise, every vendor feature below is the vendor's own claim, taken from its public pages. None of these products was bought or tested for this report.

## AI builders compete on speed while independent tests flag quality

The dedicated "answer some questions, get Excel" sites are a low-price niche. **EasyFinancialModels** comes closest to us on assurance. It claims a 16-sheet formula-linked workbook with "integrity checks" for **$29.98 per download**, and gives away models of up to 3 years ([EasyFinancialModels](https://www.easyfinancialmodels.com/)). Even so, it has no scenario toggle, and its only proof is seven Product Hunt testimonials. **ModelUp** promises three scenarios and a "fully formula-driven" file for **$4.99/mo**, but its launch drew 3 upvotes ([Hunted.space](https://www.hunted.space/product/modelup)). **FinModelBuilder** hides its price behind "pay only to download" and names no legal entity ([FinModelBuilder](https://www.finmodelbuilder.com/)). DraftPlan, our reference, promises scenarios and a validation report. Its coffee-shop sample has 42 formulas, no scenario switch and no checks tab. Its checkout is a fake door: there is no payment processor, so the button saves a lead and promises a payment link by email ([getdraftplan.com](https://getdraftplan.com/checkout.html); inspection in `docs/research/draftplan.md`). Its wizard asks only categorical questions and no numbers. As a result, "custom" there means a business-type template plus sample data.

The larger threat is the general-purpose Excel agents. **ChatGPT for Excel reached general availability on all plans on 5 May 2026** ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/blog/chatgpt-for-excel)). Claude for Excel comes with paid Claude plans from **$20/mo**, and Shortcut Pro costs **$125/mo** ([GPT for Work](https://gptforwork.com/blog/shortcut-ai-alternatives)). A finance-literate founder can now get a draft 3-statement model for the price of a monthly subscription. Independent evidence says that draft cannot be trusted as-is. Ankura ran five tools on three models: all produced structurally complete models in 7–10 minutes, and **"no tool produced a production-ready model"** ([Ankura](https://ankura.com/insights/putting-ai-to-the-test-how-5-tools-performed-across-3-financial-models)). SpreadsheetBench 2 measured the best model at **34.00% accuracy on financial-modeling tasks**, and spreadsheet products scored lower than the raw models on a hand-tested subset ([SpreadsheetBench 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29955)). In a hands-on test of Claude for Excel, the model hardcoded headcount, left out payroll burden and scattered assumptions across tabs ([GrowwStacks](https://growwstacks.com/blog/claude-ai-excel-financial-model-test/)). This is the opening for an architecture where the LLM fills only a brief, and templates plus a challenger guarantee the arithmetic. To compete with a $20 agent, Ledgerline has to sell "no prompting, no reviewing, already verified". It cannot win on "AI builds Excel".

## Templates own the $35–$210 shelf but buyers call them generic

The template market sets the price the buyer expects. Marketplace listings run **$34.95–$209.95 on eFinancialModels** and **$5–$210 on Eloquens**. Etsy shows 5,000+ "financial model template" listings, with full business models at about $23–$129 ([eFM](https://www.efinancialmodels.com/downloads/saas-financial-model-excel-template-fully-vetted-and-ready-to-use-145016/), [Eloquens](https://www.eloquens.com/), [Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/market/financial_model_template)). Below that sits a $0 floor. SCORE's free XLSX covers 3-year statements, a balance sheet, break-even and ratios, plus diagnostic range checks ([SCORE](https://www.score.org/resource/template/financial-projections-template)). Microsoft lists 13 free finance templates ([Microsoft](https://excel.cloud.microsoft/create/en/financial-management-templates/)). Specialist sellers use the template as a lead magnet and make money on services. ProjectionHub sells all 100+ templates for **$499 lifetime** and done-for-you projections for **$750**. Someka charges **$250** for basic customization ([ProjectionHub](https://projectionhub.com/pricing), [Someka](https://www.someka.net/excel-template/restaurant-financial-plan/)).

The complaints about templates are consistent. On Trustpilot, eFinancialModels buyers report sellers going unresponsive after payment and "you sold me a simple template" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/efinancialmodels.com)). FinModelsLab's negative reviews cite generic models that need heavy customization, poor niche fit, formula errors and slow support ([Trustpilot](https://ch.trustpilot.com/review/finmodelslab.com)). Someka reviewers say the model is too complex and reports refresh only on a button click ([Someka](https://www.someka.net/excel-template/restaurant-financial-plan/)). Refunds for digital templates are usually limited to defects. In short, templates are cheap and instant, but the buyer still has to do the fitting work.

## Planning SaaS rents the model, and five founder tools have disappeared

Business-plan tools are subscriptions that start with the writing. LivePlan costs **$15–$30/mo billed annually** ($20–$40 monthly) ([LivePlan](https://www.liveplan.com/pricing)). PrometAI costs $55–$145/mo and **lists no Excel export** ([PrometAI](https://prometai.app/pricing)). Where an "Export to Excel" exists, no vendor says whether the file keeps live formulas. LivePlan reviewers on Capterra complain about the "inability to move forecasts into Excel format" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/142049/LivePlan/reviews/)). The same review page shows charges after cancellation, and a CPA asking for a price aimed at people who make "five or less" projections a year. That second request is a direct signal of occasional, one-off demand ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/142049/LivePlan/reviews/)).

The self-serve founder FP&A tier has largely gone. **Finmark was sunset on 1 April 2026** and customer data became unrecoverable ([Metapraxis](https://metapraxis.com/finmark-alternatives), a competitor source; finmark.com/pricing now redirects to bill.com). **Pry was wound down by Brex in February 2026** ([MyRunwayHealth](https://myrunwayhealth.com/blog/the-best-pry-alternatives-for-founders-who-dont-have-a-finance-team), a competitor source with no primary citation). Causal now sits inside Lucanet with no public plans ([Causal](https://www.causal.app/pricing)). Mosaic became Bob Finance at HiBob. Runway rebranded as **cfo.ai on 10 September 2026**, priced at **$375–$1,500/mo** ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/10/exclusive-ai-finance-cfo-ai-launches-an-agentic-cfo-for-business-founders/), [cfo.ai](https://cfo.ai/pricing)). The tools still selling are Brixx ($25–$75/mo), Float (£99–£295/mo), and accountant-facing products such as Fathom ($450/mo for 25 companies) ([Brixx](https://www.brixx.com/pricing), [Float](https://floatapp.com/pricing), [Fathom](https://www.fathomhq.com/pricing)). For a founder who needs one model for one loan or one raise, these tools are either too expensive or too risky. The shutdowns give Ledgerline a factual message: you get an `.xlsx` you own. Ledgerline should stick to the documented dates and make no broader claims about other vendors.

## Human builders fit the business but cost 10–100x more

Fiverr is the cheapest human option. The category lists **15,217 services**, and Basic tiers cost **$10–$130** with 1–5 days' delivery ([Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/gigs/financial-model)). Basic usually means less than it sounds. One gig sold as a "3 statement" model delivers "P&L only" at Basic ($55). Another covers base case only ($80), with scenarios and valuation upsold to Premium ([rahi_sheth](https://fiverr.com/rahi_sheth/build-a-3-year-financial-model-in-excel), [caayushibansal](https://fiverr.com/caayushibansal/ca-fmva-specialist-esg-expert)). Many sellers have only 2–24 orders. Upwork's own page puts financial modelers at **$25–$65/hr** ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/hire/financial-modelers/cost/)). A pricing survey from a seller of templates (eFinancialModels) puts a freelancer's startup fundraising model at **$1,000–$4,000 over 1–4 weeks**, boutique builds at **$3,000–$25,000+**, and 3–5 revision rounds as the most common source of overruns ([eFM survey](https://www.efinancialmodels.com/?p=659204), [eFM guide](https://www.efinancialmodels.com/template-vs-custom-financial-model-pricing-guide/)). Fractional CFOs quote **$10,000–$20,000 for a 3-statement model with scenarios** ([Fiscallion](https://www.fiscallion.io/blog/fractional-cfo-cost-the-complete-pricing-guide-for-startup-founders)). At the solo-expert end, Hemrock quotes custom models at $0.5K–$10K in "a few days" ([Hemrock](https://www.hemrock.com/)).

Much of the demand comes from documents a third party requires. SBA 7(a) lenders ask for projected financial statements, including projected balance sheets ([Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/loans/small-business/startup-business-loan-documents)). E-2 visa plans need 5-year P&L, balance sheet and cash-flow projections, and a plan typically costs about $1,900 ([BSB Consulting](https://www.bsbcon.com/us/blog/how-much-does-a-business-plan-for-an-e-2-visa-cost/)). These buyers need a model with a full balance sheet and a 5-year horizon. Many of them also need a narrative plan, which Ledgerline does not produce.

## FinModelsLab overlap and cannibalisation risk

**What FinModelsLab is (measured, not marketing).** A script count of the public Shopify catalog feed shows **2,000 products**, all at **$99.00** with a $129.00 compare-at price, no tiers and no subscription ([products.json](https://finmodelslab.com/products.json?limit=250)). Every product description advertises a dashboard, a 5-year monthly forecast, break-even, payback and Low/Base/High scenario analysis. The product copy itself says the cases should be treated as manual assumption changes, not an automatic switch. In the descriptions, "balance sheet" appears in **1 of 2,000** and "sensitivity" in 85 ([products.json](https://finmodelslab.com/products.json?limit=250)). The catalog is very broad (about 542 service titles, 237 retail, 205 health, 138 food and beverage) and very niche. Refunds cover only defects, within 30 days ([refund policy](https://finmodelslab.com/policies/refund-policy)). Trustpilot shows **4.0/5 from 74 reviews, 11% one-star**. Its negative themes are generic content, rigid structure, formula errors and poor niche fit ([Trustpilot](https://ch.trustpilot.com/review/finmodelslab.com)).

**Where they overlap.** The overlap in headline claims is almost total. Both offer one-off Excel, 5-year monthly forecasts, Low/Base/High scenarios, a dashboard and break-even. Both target SMB founders who need a model for a lender or investor. Both reach buyers through SEO on "[industry] financial model" queries. To a buyer skimming the two sites, the products look like substitutes. If Ledgerline is priced at or below $99, it competes head-on with FinModelsLab for the same search click. It would also win on the very points where FinModelsLab gets bad reviews: fit to the buyer's business, formula errors and generic content. That is the cannibalisation case. At that price, Ledgerline would take sales from FinModelsLab instead of growing Henry's total revenue.

**Where they don't overlap.** FinModelsLab sells breadth, about 2,000 niches available right away. Ledgerline starts with a few templates (the PoC is a cafe) that can be configured in depth. FinModelsLab gives the buyer a niche template to fill in. Ledgerline gives the buyer a model built on their own key numbers, with a one-cell scenario switch, a Checks sheet that must not FAIL, a full three-statement structure, and an independent recompute. None of these is visible in FinModelsLab's public descriptions. Whether the purchased FinModelsLab files contain a balance sheet or checks is unverified.

**Implications for pricing.** Ledgerline should be priced clearly above the $99 FinModelsLab shelf price, and below the $250–$750 entry price for human customization (Someka $250, ProjectionHub $750). That makes it the premium step up from a template, not a cheaper option beside it. It also leaves DraftPlan's $49/$59 to compete on price alone. A price close to $99 is the worst choice, because it maximises substitution and signals that Ledgerline is just another template. The exact figure stays [PRICE] until Henry decides. A "start from a FinModelsLab template and pay the difference to have it built on your numbers" upgrade credit would turn the overlap into a funnel. That is a strategic call for Henry.

**Implications for positioning.** Ledgerline's message must not run down templates in general ("templates are generic"), because that criticises FinModelsLab too. Position Ledgerline on what it adds: your numbers already in, one switch for Low/Base/High, checks that pass, and numbers recomputed independently. Launch SEO should avoid the "[niche] financial model template" pages FinModelsLab already targets. Better targets are intent-heavy queries such as "SBA loan projections", "E-2 visa financial projections" and "investor model with scenarios", plus the few industries Ledgerline actually supports. FinModelsLab's 2,000 product pages could later carry a "have this built for your numbers" link to Ledgerline. Keeping the brands separate protects FinModelsLab's catalog from being seen as the "lesser" product. Do not reuse FinModelsLab copy on Ledgerline pages.

## 5 gaps we can own

### 1. Proven arithmetic, not "AI-built"
No competitor publishes an independent recompute of its own output. Ankura found that no AI tool produced a production-ready model ([Ankura](https://ankura.com/insights/putting-ai-to-the-test-how-5-tools-performed-across-3-financial-models)). SpreadsheetBench 2 measured 34% top accuracy on financial-modeling tasks ([SpreadsheetBench 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29955)). Template buyers report formula errors ([Trustpilot](https://ch.trustpilot.com/review/finmodelslab.com)). DraftPlan's samples have no checks tab (`docs/research/draftplan.md`). EasyFinancialModels claims "integrity checks", but shows no recompute ([EasyFinancialModels](https://www.easyfinancialmodels.com/)). A challenger match within $1 in every scenario-year can be stated as a verifiable fact. Competitors have only claims.

### 2. The buyer's own numbers at a template-like price
There is a price gap between $35–$210 templates and $750+ done-for-you projections ([ProjectionHub](https://projectionhub.com/pricing)) or $1,000–$4,000 freelancers ([eFM survey](https://www.efinancialmodels.com/?p=659204)). The AI builders that sit in that gap either take no numbers (DraftPlan's wizard is categorical only) or prove nothing. The top template complaint is "generic, needs customization" ([Trustpilot](https://ch.trustpilot.com/review/finmodelslab.com)). A key-numbers step in the wizard fills this gap directly.

### 3. A real one-cell Low/Base/High switch inside the file
FinModelsLab's own copy describes its cases as manual assumption changes ([products.json](https://finmodelslab.com/products.json?limit=250)). Fiverr Basic tiers are base case only ([caayushibansal](https://fiverr.com/caayushibansal/ca-fmva-specialist-esg-expert)). SaaS tools keep scenarios in the web app, and their Excel exports are of unclear fidelity ([Capterra LivePlan](https://www.capterra.com/p/142049/LivePlan/reviews/)). DraftPlan's coffee-shop sample has no switch. A switch that works in the delivered `.xlsx` is rare enough to put in the headline.

### 4. Pay once and own the file
Reviewers report renewals and charges they did not expect on LivePlan and Upmetrics, and one asks for low-volume pricing ([Capterra LivePlan](https://www.capterra.com/p/142049/LivePlan/reviews/), [Trustpilot Upmetrics](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/upmetrics.co)). When Finmark and Pry shut down, customers lost models built inside those tools ([Metapraxis](https://metapraxis.com/finmark-alternatives), [MyRunwayHealth](https://myrunwayhealth.com/blog/the-best-pry-alternatives-for-founders-who-dont-have-a-finance-team)). The surviving SaaS tools cost $25–$1,500/mo. For a founder who needs one model for one loan or raise, a one-time `.xlsx` with live formulas solves a documented problem.

### 5. Honest, inspectable proof
Proof is thin across the niche. EasyFinancialModels shows first-name testimonials, FinModelBuilder unverified names, ModelUp 3 upvotes, Fiverr sellers 2–24 orders, and DraftPlan has no payment processor ([EasyFinancialModels](https://www.easyfinancialmodels.com/), [FinModelBuilder](https://www.finmodelbuilder.com/), [Hunted.space](https://www.hunted.space/product/modelup), [Fiverr](https://fiverr.com/actprofessional/build-financial-modeling-projections-financial-analysis-for-your-business)). Ledgerline can publish what nobody else does: a downloadable sample with its formula count, the Checks sheet result and the challenger's match report. That proof needs no testimonials and no invented statistics.

## Conclusion

The hard part of the market is a combination, and nobody covers all of it. Some competitors are cheap, some fit the buyer's business, some are verified, and some let the buyer own the file. The AI builders closest to Ledgerline are cheap but unproven. The tools that do fit the business cost ten times more and take weeks. Real proof, rather than features, is therefore Ledgerline's main asset: a published sample with its Checks result and challenger match does more than any feature list, because the niche is full of claims nobody can check. The main danger to Henry's revenue is FinModelsLab, not DraftPlan. Pricing above $99, positioning on additions rather than template-bashing, and a later upgrade path from FinModelsLab's 2,000 product pages would turn an overlap that could cannibalise sales into a funnel between the two businesses.
